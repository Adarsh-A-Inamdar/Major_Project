CHAPTER 1 
INTRODUCTION 
Leukemia is a serious type of blood cancer that originates in the bone marrow, the primary site 
of blood cell production. It is characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal white 
blood cells, which interferes with the normal production of red blood cells, healthy white blood 
cells, and platelets. As a result, patients may experience anemia, recurrent infections, fatigue, 
and excessive bleeding or bruising. Leukemia is primarily caused by genetic mutations in 
hematopoietic cells, while risk fa ctors such as radiation exposure, toxic chemicals, smoking, 
and previous cancer therapies may also contribute to its development. Early detection and 
accurate grading of leukemia are crucial for effective treatment planning and improved patient 
survival rates [1], [2]. 
Traditional leukemia diagnosis relies heavily on manual examination of peripheral blood smear 
images, bone marrow biopsies, and laboratory tests performed by experienced 
hematopathologists. Although these methods are considered reliable, they are time-consuming, 
labor-intensive, and subject to inter-observer variability. With the increasing global incidence 
of leukemia and the shortage of skilled medical professionals, there is a growing demand for 
automated and efficient diagnostic systems to assist clinic ians in early disease detection and 
classification [3]. 
Recent advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) and deep learning (DL) have significantly 
improved medical image analysis. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have demonstrated 
excellent performance in automated feature extraction and classification t asks. Among CNN 
architectures, Residual Networks (ResNet) address the vanishing gradient problem through 
skip connections, enabling deeper networks to learn more effective feature representations. 
ResNet-18, a lightweight yet powerful CNN model, offers a suitable balance between accuracy 
and computational efficiency, making it well -suited for medical image analysis applications 
[4]. The performance of deep learning models is highly influenced by optimal parameter 
selection and effective feature representati on. To enhance model accuracy and robustness, 
metaheuristic optimization techniques have gained increasing attention. Particle Swarm 
Optimization (PSO), inspired by the social behaviour of bird flocks, is an efficient optimization

1.2 Domain Overview  
Leukemia is a type of blood cancer that originates in the bone marrow, where blood cells are 
produced. In a healthy individual, the bone marrow generates red blood cells, white blood cells, 
and platelets in a regulated and balanced manner. In leukemia, gen etic mutations occur in 
hematopoietic stem cells, leading to the uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal white blood 
cells. These leukemic cells are immature, non-functional, and gradually replace healthy blood 
cells, resulting in serious clinical conditions such as anemia, recurrent infections, fatigue, and 
excessive bleeding or bruising. Due to its aggressive nature, particularly in acute forms, 
leukemia requires early detection and accurate classification to improve patient survival rates 
[9], [10]. 
The detection of leukemia  primarily involves clinical evaluation supported by laboratory 
investigations. A complete blood count (CBC) is usually the first diagnostic test, often 
revealing abnormal white blood cell counts, reduced red blood cell levels, or 
thrombocytopenia. This is  followed by microscopic examination of peripheral blood smear 
images to analyse blood cell morphology. In many cases, a bone marrow biopsy is performed 
to confirm the diagnosis and assess disease severity. Advanced diagnostic techniques such as 
immunophenotyping, cytogenetic analysis, and molecular testing are further employed to 
identify specific leukemia subtypes and genetic abnormalities, which are crucial for accurate 
diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment planning [11], [12].

2.1 Review of Research Papers: 
1. PSO -Based Leukemia Classification Using CNN Hyperparameter Optimization 
(2024): This study introduces a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) framework to enhance the 
performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) used for leukemia classification. PSO 
is leveraged to automatically tune hyperparameters such as learning rate, number of  
convolutional layers, filter sizes, and batch size. The system was tested on the ALL -IDB 
dataset, focusing on acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) classification. The PSO -optimized 
CNN outperformed manually-tuned counterparts in terms of accuracy, convergence speed, and 
training efficiency. Moreover, PSO reduced the need for exhaustive trial -and-error 
experiments, saving both time and computational resources. Despite performan ce gains, the 
black-box nature of CNNs and lack of interpretability pose challenges for clinical acceptance, 
requiring integration with explainable AI tools for deployment.[16] 
2. Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) for Feature Selection in Leukemia Subtype 
Classification (2023) : The paper explores the use of Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) for 
selecting the most relevant features from blood smear image datasets before classification with 
a deep neural network (DNN). ACO mimics the behavior of ants searching for optimal paths 
and is effective in reducing feature space dimensionality, which improves the model's training 
speed and classification accuracy. The proposed model was evaluated on a small clinical dataset 
and showed strong performance in identifying leukemia subtypes like AML and ALL.  The 
study is constrained by a limited sample size, raising concerns about overfitting and 
generalizability. The authors advocate for future trials on larger, multi -center datasets to 
validate robustness.[17]

Expression Profiles (2022) : This research presents a hybrid approach where Genetic 
Algorithms (GAs) are used to select informative genes from microarray gene expression data, 
followed by classification using a Support Vector Machine (SVM). GA was successful in 
identifying a minimal set of genes most predictive of leukemia, which significantly improved 
model sensitivity, precision, and computational speed. The model was particularly effective in

2.3 Existing system 
Existing leukemia diagnostic models generally address either classification or grading 
independently, with no integrated approach combining both critical tasks in a unified 
framework. These systems often struggle with challenges such as class imbalance in datasets, 
limited interpretability of results, and inadequate clinical validation or workflow integration. 
Additionally, most current methods do not utilize metaheuristic optimization techniques, which 
have the potential to improve diagnostic accuracy by e ffectively optimizing feature selection 
and model parameters. This lack of metaheuristic-based approaches leaves room for enhancing 
performance and reliability. Therefore, there is a clear need for developing a comprehensive 
model that simultaneously perfo rms leukemia classification and grading while employing 
metaheuristic optimization to maximize accuracy and clinical relevance. 
2.4 Problem Statement 
Leukemia diagnosis requires precise classification and grading to guide effective treatment 
decisions. Existing automated systems predominantly focus on either classification or grading 
in isolation, lacking an integrated approach that addresses both simultaneously. Moreover, the 
accuracy and robustness of these systems are often compromised due to challenges such as 
imbalanced datasets, high -dimensional feature spaces, and limited interpretability. Current 
models rarely employ metaheuristic optimization te chniques, which can enhance model 
performance by effectively selecting relevant features and tuning parameters. This gap results 
in suboptimal diagnostic outcomes and limited clinical applicability. Therefore, there is a 
critical need to develop a metaheuristic-driven framework that concurrently performs leukemia 
classification and grading, optimizing diagnostic accuracy and providing reliable, interpretable 
results suitable for clinical integration. 
2.5 Proposed System 
Existing leukemia diagnostic approaches often handle classification and grading as 
independent tasks and directly apply deep learning models on high -dimensional medical 
images. Such methods may suffer from redundant feature learning, increased computationa l 
cost, and reduced robustness when dealing with noisy data and class imbalance commonly 
found in blood smear images. Moreover, without prior optimization, deep learning models may 
not fully capture the most discriminative features required for reliable leukemia analysis.

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE 
4.1 Overview of the System Architecture  
The proposed system architecture is designed for effective leukemia classification and severity 
grading using microscopic blood smear images by integrating metaheuristic optimization and 
deep learning. Unlike conventional pipelines where deep learning prec edes optimization, this 
system first applies Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to guide optimal feature relevance and 
learning configuration, followed by ResNet -18 for deep feature extraction and classification. 
This ordering improves learning efficiency and enhances overall system performance. 
The architecture follows a sequential and modular flow starting from image acquisition, 
preprocessing, optimization using PSO, deep feature extraction using ResNet -18, and finally 
classification and grading. Each module is independently designed to ensure scalability, clarity, 
and robustness.  
 
4.2 System Architecture 
The proposed system begins with microscopic blood smear images obtained from standard 
datasets such as ALL -IDB, C -NMC, and BCCD. These images are first passed through an 
image preprocessing module, where noise is removed, color variations are normalized, a nd 
images are resized to a standard format to ensure consistency and improve visual quality. The 
preprocessed images are then processed using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), a 
metaheuristic optimization technique used to identify relevant features and o ptimize learning 
parameters for improved model performance. Following optimization, deep feature extraction 
is carried out using the ResNet -18 convolutional neural network, which effectively captures 
important characteristics such as cell shape, texture, a nd nucleus structure. The optimized 
training model with minimum error configuration is then selected and used to classify leukemia 
into major types including ALL, AML, CLL, and CML, along with grading the severity into 
Grade-1, Grade-2, and Grade-3. Finally, the system’s performance is evaluated using standard 
metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 -score to assess the reliability and 
effectiveness of the proposed approach.

4.3 Role of Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) 
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is applied as an initial optimization stage in the proposed 
system to improve the effectiveness of the deep learning process. PSO begins by initializing a 
swarm of particles, where each particle represents a possible solut ion in terms of feature 
relevance weighting and learning configuration parameters. These particles explore the search

space simultaneously and are evaluated using a fitness function that measures how well the 
selected feature configuration supports accurate learning. During each iteration, particles 
update their velocities and positions based on both their individual best  solutions (personal

strategy enables PSO to efficiently explore a large and complex solution space, gradually 
converging toward an optimal configuration that highlights the most informative features while

By applying PSO prior to the ResNet-18 deep learning stage, the system ensures that the deep 
network is guided toward meaningful and discriminative patterns within blood smear images. 
The optimized feature weighting allows ResNet -18 to focus on critical re gions and relevant 
characteristics such as cell morphology, nucleus structure, and texture variations. This prior 
optimization reduces unnecessary feature learning, improves convergence speed, and enhances 
training stability. As a result, the deep learning  model achieves better generalization 
performance and higher accuracy in leukemia classification and severity grading, making the 
overall system more efficient and reliable for medical diagnostic applications. 
 
4.4 Role of ResNet-18 in the Architecture 
After the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) stage completes the optimization of feature 
relevance and learning configuration, the optimized data representation is forwarded to the 
ResNet-18 deep convolutional neural network for further processing. ResNet -18 utilizes a 
layered architecture composed of convolutional blocks and residual (skip) connections, 
enabling hierarchical feature extraction from blood smear images. These layers progressively 
learn low-level features such as edges and textures, followed b y higher-level features such as 
cell shape, nucleus structure, and morphological variations that are critical for leukemia 
identification. 
The optimized configuration generated by PSO enhances the learning capability of ResNet-18 
by guiding the network to focus on informative and discriminative patterns. The residual 
learning mechanism in ResNet -18 ensures stable gradient flow and prevents pe rformance 
degradation as the network depth increases. As a result, the network achieves faster 
convergence, improved stability, and stronger generalization performance. This combination 
enables accurate leukemia classification and effective severity gradin g, making ResNet -18 a 
reliable core component in the proposed metaheuristic deep learning architecture.

 Fig 5.2 ResNet-18 Layer 
 
From fig 5.2 the ResNet-18 is a deep convolutional neural network with 18 layers designed 
using residual (skip) connections. These skip connections allow the network to pass input 
features directly to deeper layers, helping to avoid the vanishing gradient problem and enabling 
stable training of deeper models. The architecture mainly consists of multiple 3×3 
convolutional layers grouped into residual blocks, followed by batch normalization and ReLU 
activation. At the end, an average pooling layer, a fully connected (FC) laye r, and a softmax 
layer are used to generate class probabilities. 
 
In our leukemia classification and grading project, ResNet -18 plays a crucial role as a feature 
extractor for microscopic blood smear images. It effectively learns important visual patterns 
such as cell shape, nucleus structure, texture, and color variations that distinguish normal cells 
from leukemic cells. The residual connections ensure that fine -grained features are preserved 
across layers, which is essential for medical images where differences are often subtle. The 
deep features extracted by ResNet -18 are then optimized using Particle Swarm Optimization 
(PSO) to enhance classification accuracy and robustness.

The following sections illustrate the logical flow of the system, from handling initial inputs to 
the final classification and grading of the leukemia cells. 
1. Preprocessing & Input Handling 
Before any analysis can occur, the raw image data must be standardized. This process ensures 
that every image fed into the model is consistent in size and format. 
BEGIN 
    Load the image from its source (File Path or User Upload) 
    Resize the image to a standard 224 × 224 pixel resolution 
    Convert the image color mode to RGB 
    Transform the image data into a Tensor format 
    Normalize pixel values using standard ImageNet statistics: 
        Mean: [0.485, 0.456, 0.406] 
        Standard Deviation: [0.229, 0.224, 0.225] 
    Return the fully Preprocessed Tensor 
END 
2. Metaheuristic Optimization (PSO) 
To achieve the best possible performance, we employ Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) to 
fine-tune our hyperparameters. This algorithm mimics the behavior of a swarm to find the 
optimal learning rate and batch size. 
In this project, the Batch Size is optimized by Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). 
1. Velocity Update: 
v(t+1) = w * v(t) + c1 * r1 * (pbest - x(t)) + c2 * r2 * (gbest - x(t)) 
2. Position Update: 
x(t+1) = x(t) + v(t+1) 
3. Batch Size Mapping:

Department of CSE, BIET DAVANGERE                                                                                              21 
 
Batch Size = Round( x_batch_dimension(t+1) ) 
BEGIN PSO 
    Initialize the search boundaries (Lower and Upper Bounds) for: 
        - Learning Rate 
        - Batch Size 
    Define Objective_Function(Hyperparameters): 
        Initialize a temporary Model with these parameters 
        Train for a short duration ('Tuning Epochs') 
        Return the resulting Validation Loss 
    Initialize a Swarm of particles 
    FOR each iteration DO 
        FOR each particle DO 
            Evaluate current position using Objective_Function 
            Update the particle's Personal Best score 
        END FOR 
        Update the swarm's Global Best score 
    END FOR 
    Return the Best Hyperparameters found (Lowest Loss) 
END PSO 
3. Multi-Task ResNet18 Model Design 
The core intelligence of the system is a modified ResNet18 architecture. Unlike a standard 
classifier, this model is designed to perform two tasks simultaneously: identifying the type of 
leukemia and determining its severity grade. 
BEGIN MODEL

    Discard the original final fully-connected layer 
    Attach Head-1: A Linear layer specifically for predicting Leukemia Type (ALL, AML, 
CLL, CML) 
    Attach Head-2: A Linear layer specifically for predicting Grade (Chronic, Accelerated, 
Blast) 
    Function Forward(Input Image): 
        Extract Features using the Backbone

4. Training Process 
The training loop is where the model learns from the data. It iteratively adjusts its weights to 
minimize error for both classification and grading tasks. 
BEGIN TRAINING 
    Retrieve Optimal Hyperparameters from the PSO phase 
    Initialize the Multi-Task Model 
    Define Loss Functions for both tasks: 
        Loss_Type = CrossEntropyLoss 
        Loss_Grade = CrossEntropyLoss 
    FOR each Epoch DO 
        // Training Phase 
        FOR each Batch in the Training_Set DO 
            Generate Predictions (Type and Grade) from Model 
            Calculate Total_Loss = Loss_Type + (0.5 * Loss_Grade) 
            Backpropagate the error to update weights 
        END FOR   
        // Validation Phase 
        Evaluate Model performance on Validation Set 
        IF Accuracy improves THEN 
            Save the current Model Weights to 'multitask_model.pt' 
        END IF 
    END FOR 
END TRAINING

CONCLUSION 
 
Leukemia is a life -threatening blood cancer in which early and accurate diagnosis is 
essential for improving patient survival and treatment outcomes. This project presents an 
intelligent and integrated framework that combines Particle Swarm Optimization (P SO) 
with the ResNet-18 deep learning architecture to effectively classify leukemia types and 
grade disease severity. By applying PSO as a preliminary optimization stage, the system 
identifies relevant feature patterns and optimizes learning configurations before deep 
feature extraction, enabling ResNet -18 to focus on meaningful and discriminative 
characteristics such as cell morphology, nucleus structure, and texture variations in blood 
smear images. The residual learning capability of ResNet-18 ensures stable training, faster 
convergence, and improved generalization performance across multiple datasets. Overall, 
the proposed PSO –ResNet-18 framework provides a reliable, efficient, and practical 
decision-support solution that assists healthcare professionals in achieving accurate 
leukemia diagnosis and severity assessment, ultimately contributing to better clinical 
decision-making and improved patient care.

LIMITATION AND FUTURE SCOPE 
 
Limitations 
Although the proposed PSO –ResNet-18 based system demonstrates strong performance in 
leukemia classification and grading, certain limitations remain. The model relies primarily on 
microscopic blood smear images, and variations in staining quality, image res olution, and 
acquisition conditions can influence performance. ResNet-18, while computationally efficient, 
has a relatively shallow depth compared to deeper architectures, which may limit its ability to 
capture very complex and fine -grained patterns in som e challenging cases. Additionally, the 
optimization process using PSO increases training time due to iterative evaluations, and the 
system’s performance may vary depending on dataset size and class distribution. 
Interpretability, although improved through optimized feature learning, still requires further 
enhancement for full clinical transparency. 
 
Future Scope 
• Deeper ResNet Models: 
Using deeper architectures such as ResNet-34, ResNet-50, or ResNet-101 can help the 
model learn more detailed and complex patterns from blood smear images, leading to 
improved feature extraction and higher classification accuracy. 
• Advanced Optimization Techniques: 
Exploring hybrid or more efficient metaheuristic optimization methods can reduce 
training time while further enhancing model performance and stability. 
• Multi-Modal Data Integration: 
Combining blood smear images with additional clinical information, such as 
laboratory test results or genetic data, can provide a more complete understanding of 
the disease and improve diagnostic reliability. 
• Real-Time Clinical Deployment: 
Deploying the system in real clinical environments can enable large-scale, real-time 
leukemia screening and support timely diagnosis and decision-making.
